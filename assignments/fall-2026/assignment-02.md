**Predict → Build It → Use It → Ship It → Verify**

Bundle [Lesson 3](../../lessons/03-archetype-and-positioning/README.md) and [Lesson 4](../../lessons/04-architecture-and-naming/README.md) into one contribution to your running brand project. The lesson Assessments are ungraded.

## Predict

Which positioning decision will force you to decline an otherwise attractive feature or message? Will your product names make the relationship to the parent brand clear? Identify likely ambiguity. Save the expectation before the relevant output or feedback.

## Build It

Build a Python strategy checker for audience, category, benefit, reason to believe, voice principles, and explicit refusals. Use archetypes as optional creative heuristics rather than psychological diagnoses.

Build a Python brand-tree validator and name-candidate ledger. Detect duplicate IDs, cycles, missing parent references, and normalized name collisions within the classroom inventory.

## Use It

Ask Claude for three positioning alternatives and a counterexample to each. Select one and document why the other two fit less well.

Ask Claude to generate names constrained by your strategy. Compare branded-house and separate-brand structures. Record availability and legal clearance as unverified unless actually established through an appropriate process.

## Ship It

Submit positioning statement, strategy configuration, checker, and rejected alternatives; brand tree, candidate names, validation output, and unresolved clearance questions. Include the implementation, tests, inputs, actual outputs, source credits, and [submission records](../../templates/README.md). Post the final checked version to the designated GitHub location and upload the matching version to Canvas.

## Verify

Test contradictory benefit/constraint pairs and unsupported reasons to believe. A consistent configuration still requires audience evidence.

Test a cycle, missing parent, and duplicate normalized name. Domain availability, classroom uniqueness, and legal rights are different questions; this exercise does not establish trademark clearance.

Document actual findings and limitations. Fictional cases, simulations, and proposed campaigns must remain labeled. No ad spend, public release, invented testimonial, or legal clearance is required or implied.

## Division of labor

**AI should** generate and compare strategic alternatives; generate candidates and inspect structural consistency.

**Human should** select and defend the positioning and the tradeoffs it creates; select the architecture and names and identify unresolved rights or review needs.
