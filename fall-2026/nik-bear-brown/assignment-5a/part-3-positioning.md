# Assignment 5A, Part 3 — Competitive Positioning (25 pts)

## A. Positioning matrix (12 pts)

Axes chosen for what actually matters to the Part 2 user — the independent
consultant who is *not* job hunting, but deciding *who to pitch*:

- **Axis 1 (horizontal): who the hiring data serves** — *Applicant* ("find me
  a job") → *Advisor* ("read what the company cares about").
- **Axis 2 (vertical): teaching specificity** — *General hiring data* →
  *Teaching-role evidence* (enablement, training, academy, advocacy roles
  parsed as pedagogy interest).

```
Teaching-role
evidence  ^
          |        . Lectern
          |        (advisor × teaching:
          |         reads teaching hiring
          |         as pedagogy interest,
          |         shows the rule)
          |
          |  . Scoutify / HireSignal
          |  (advisor × general:
          |   hiring as BUYING intent
          |   for sales teams)
          |
          +----------------------------------> who the data serves
          |  . LinkedIn        Applicant      Advisor
          |  Job Alerts
          |  . HiringCafe
          |  . TrueUp
          |  (applicant × general:
          v   match titles to seekers)
General
hiring data
```

- **LinkedIn Job Alerts** — bottom-left. Largest pool, best alerts; but every
  alert matches *titles for applicants*. No demand view, no evidence trail.
  Our user isn't applying.
- **HiringCafe** — bottom-left. Aggregator for seekers; same applicant framing.
- **TrueUp** — bottom-left. Tech-job matching for applicants; general data.
- **Scoutify / HireSignal** (comparators, from Brief Part 6) — top-left. They
  *do* read listings as evidence of company interest — but as **buying**
  intent, for sales teams. Scoutify proves the plumbing (70k+ boards,
  Greenhouse/Lever/Ashby) is reachable; HireSignal claims 60–90-day lead on
  vendor research. Neither parses teaching-related hiring, neither serves the
  consultant.
- **Lectern** — top-right. The only advisor-framed reader aimed at pedagogy
  interest, with the rule trace per posting.

**The white space:** advisor × teaching-specific. It exists because the two
adjacent categories grew up serving someone else: job boards serve seekers,
sales-intel serves sellers. Nobody built the hiring-as-evidence reader for the
person selling *teaching* — the consultant whose target role is rarely posted
at all. Competitors don't occupy it because their business model is
applicant-side (ads, applications) or sales-side (lead gen); checkable
pedagogy evidence is a third job nobody monetized.

## B. Refined one-thing statement (13 pts)

**Original one-thing (Conductor Brief Part 6B, verbatim):**

> Lectern is the only job-board reader that turns listings into checkable
> evidence of a company's interest in teaching, for the independent education
> consultant, without asking them to pretend to be a job seeker.

**Revised one-thing (PROVISIONAL — validate in testing):**

> Lectern is the only hiring-data tool that reads teaching-related postings as
> checkable evidence of a company's interest in pedagogy, for the independent
> education consultant, without making them pose as an applicant or trust an
> opaque score.

**Why this revision matters (provisional reasoning, to check against tests):**
The original says "job-board reader" — testers may not think of themselves as
reading job boards at all; "hiring-data tool" meets them where the value is.
"Without asking them to pretend to be a job seeker" names the indignity every
competitor imposes (LinkedIn, HiringCafe, TrueUp all require applicant-mode);
the revision sharpens it to "pose as an applicant or trust an opaque score"
because Scoutify/HireSignal — the honest comparators — don't require
applicant-mode, they just answer a different question opaquely. If testing
shows users don't care about the rule trace, this revision is wrong and gets
rewritten from what they actually said. The test is the boss.
