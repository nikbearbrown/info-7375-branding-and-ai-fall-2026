# Reject audit — Lectern run of 2026-09-26

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A reviewable sample of the **3,358 postings Lectern rejected** on 2026-09-26, so a person can answer the one question no script can: *did the filter miss anything?*

**Why read it.** Every accuracy number so far describes what the filter *kept*. Nothing has measured what it threw away. Until someone reads rejects, the collector has no false-negative rate in either direction — and a filter that quietly drops the right jobs looks identical to one that works.

**How to use it.** Scan the titles. In the Verdict column write **MISS** if the posting should have been kept, **ok** if rejecting it was right, and **?** if you cannot tell without opening the link. Leave a note on anything surprising. Fill in Part 1 first: it is the only part that supports a rate.

**Two parts.** Part 1 is a **uniform random sample of 100** drawn from all 3,358 rejects with seed `20260926` — rerunning the script reproduces exactly this list. Part 2 is **every one of the 63 closest calls**, the rejects that mentioned two teaching words without clearing the bar of three. Part 1 gives the rate; Part 2 is where a miss is most likely to be hiding. A further 950 rejects matched exactly one teaching word — too many to read and too thin to be worth it, so their count is reported rather than their contents.

**Read the reason it was rejected as a warning, not a verdict.** A posting rejected for `no-keyword-match` can still be a job about teaching — the filter matches strings, and a Technical Curriculum Lead whose ad never uses any of the words is invisible to it.

## Two things the snippets exposed before anyone read a row

Both are for you to rule on. Neither has been changed in `keywords.json`.

### 1. `education` is in 618 of 618 Anthropic postings

Every Anthropic ad ends with the same Logistics block: *"Minimum education: Bachelor's degree or an equivalent combination of education, training, and/or experience."* So the topic word **`education` carries no information on that board** — it is boilerplate, exactly like the three words already ignored on Writer's board. It is visible in the snippets below on Data Engineer, Product Designer, Recruiting Analytics Data Engineer, and every other Anthropic reject.

This is most of why **950 rejects matched exactly one teaching word**: the word was `education`, in a salary-and-logistics footer.

**The call for you:** add `education` to `ignore_words_by_company` for Anthropic. It would change Anthropic's counts, which is why it is not done. `educate` and `teach` are unaffected — those appear in real sentences.

### 2. The topic-word rule is keeping recruiters, not teachers

79 of the 88 kept records were kept on a **role word in the title**. The other **9 were kept on topic words alone**, and most of them look wrong:

| Kept on topic words alone | Words that did it | Looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic — Head of Technical Training | curriculum, education, teach, classroom | **a real hit** |
| Replit — Japan Growth Lead | universities, curriculum, education | maybe — sells into universities |
| Anthropic — Partnership Manager, AI for Science | universities, student, education | maybe |
| Anthropic — Technical Architect | educate, education, teach | probably not |
| Stripe — University Recruiter ×3 | university, campus, student, education | **campus recruiting, not teaching** |
| Notion — Head of Early Career Recruiting | university, universities, campus | **campus recruiting** |
| OpenAI — Integrated Marketing Manager, Youth Culture | campus, student, education | **marketing** |

`university`, `campus`, and `student` are **recruiting vocabulary as much as teaching vocabulary**, and on this evidence they pull in more recruiters than teachers. That is a false-positive problem, which this audit was not even looking for — it was looking for false negatives.

**The call for you:** whether `university`, `campus`, and `student` stay as topic words, move to a lower weight, or require pairing with a genuine teaching word (`curriculum`, `teach`, `classroom`) before they count.
