## Part 1 — Expanded brand story, 300 words (25 pts)

**Framework: The Quest.** *Setup → rising action → turning point → resolution → takeaway.*

> **Setup.** I teach AI tools to students, and I wanted to teach them for the companies that make them: part-time, on flexible terms, without leaving the classroom. Figma was the obvious first stop, because I use it to teach.
>
> **Rising action.** I did not scroll the careers page. I wrote a program that fetched the whole board, saved it unchanged, and showed me every posting it kept and the words that made it keep it. The result was plain. Two Designer Advocate roles, both full time, none mentioning a university, none flexible. So I widened the net, first to seven companies, then to eighteen on three different hiring systems, and it kept three thousand four hundred forty-six postings and picked out ninety-seven.
>
> **Turning point.** Then I read what it had thrown away. The role closest to my work, Replit's Learning Experiences Creator, was in the reject pile because my keyword list said "learning designer," not "learning experiences." One word had hidden a bullseye. And when I audited the filter, my audit made its own mistake: it counted machine-learning "training" jobs as teaching jobs. The tool had been right and the check had been wrong.
>
> **Resolution.** I changed the list, fixed the audit, changed the target from "advocate roles" to "any job whose product is teaching materials," and ranked companies instead of postings, because a company that has decided teaching is worth paying for will make a role. Every change is in a public log, including the ones that went wrong.
>
> **Takeaway.** If you are looking for work that does not have a posting yet, the useful question is which companies invest in what you do. And if you use AI to look, make it show its work, because the answer will read well whether it is right or not.

*Events (record):* the sources named under Story 1 and 2, and `../build-your-data-pipeline/company-demand-2026-09-26.md` for the shift to ranking companies.

## Part 2 — The article, 500 words minimum (40 pts)

**Headline (draft):** "I searched eighteen job boards for the job that doesn't exist yet"

**Framework:** The Quest, visible as the subheads.

**Draft (judgment; every event is record):**

> ## The question
>
> I wanted to teach for a company whose tools I already teach, part-time, while staying a professor. The obvious move was to look at Figma's job board. So I did, but not by scrolling.
>
> ## The first answer was no
>
> I wrote a program that fetched the board, saved the raw response, and kept the postings about teaching, with the words that matched shown next to each. On the day I ran it, Figma had two Designer Advocate roles, both full time, none mentioning a university, none offering flexible terms. The board had answered.
>
> ## So I looked everywhere
>
> One company is an anecdote. I widened the search to eighteen boards on three hiring systems, three thousand four hundred forty-six postings, and kept ninety-seven. Then the interesting part started: I read the ones the program had thrown away.
>
> ## The bullseye hiding behind one word
>
> Replit's Learning Experiences Creator was in the reject pile. My list had "learning designer," not "learning experiences." My audit of the filter then made its own mistake, counting machine-learning "training" jobs as education. The filter had been right and the check had been wrong. Both errors were written into the log.
>
> ## How I stopped trusting my own list
>
> After that I changed how I worked. Every posting the program keeps shows the words that matched and where. Every response from a company's board is saved, dated, before anything reads it, so any verdict can be re-run and checked. A random sample of rejects is drawn so a person can read it. A second script groups every title by the kind of job it names and shows me only the places where the filter and the job family disagree. None of this makes the list correct. It makes the list arguable, which is the most a filter can be.
>
> ## What the data was actually telling me
>
> It was never going to hand me a part-time advocate job. Those are rarely posted. What it could show was which companies had decided that teaching is worth paying for: Anthropic showed all five kinds of evidence I counted (education sales, internal enablement, advocacy, teaching, an education product team), and a few others showed three. A company like that will make a role for the right person. So the list became a list of people to talk to, not postings to apply to.
>
> ## What I would tell you
>
> If you are looking for work that has no posting, count what companies invest in, not what they advertise. And if you let a machine do the looking, make it show its work. A polished list is not a trustworthy one, and the way to tell the difference is to read what it rejected.
>
> I have not yet had the conversations this list is meant to start, so I cannot tell you how they go. What I can tell you is that the search changed the question I was asking, from "which job do I apply to" to "who is already paying for what I do, and how would I show them." That is a better question, and I would not have found it by scrolling a careers page.
