## Main-book adaptations

Lessons 1–4 establish the brief, audience, positioning, structure, and names (Chapters 1–8). Lessons 5–8 develop identity, voice, experience, and campaigns (9–12). Lessons 9–13 address evidence, governance, ethics, crisis response, and cultural adaptation (13–17). Lessons 14–15 turn the work into a portfolio and handoff (18–19).

Chapters 20–23 are optional Ship It product-track readings. Their product scoping and pipeline concepts may help a capstone, but required mechanisms remain Python. The existing Madison and non-Python application scaffolds are not course prerequisites. The book's chapter points, publishing instructions, and earlier syllabi do not supersede the new Fall 2026 Assignment briefs.

Archetypes are design heuristics, not diagnostic facts about customers. Claims about universal audience behavior, fixed campaign effects, or cultural groups should be treated as hypotheses requiring evidence. A name ledger does not establish trademark clearance; a claim-proof map does not establish legal compliance. Consult current authoritative information when an actual rights or regulatory question arises. This conversion does not endorse the manuscript's dated legal statements as current advice.

A fictional customer, simulated reviewer, or synthetic campaign table must stay labeled. No assignment requires actual ad spending, public release, or fabricated feedback. Original manuscript and media assets remain unchanged.
